# Live overlays: the kick-off pop-up and line-ups, two styles each

The family's own broadcast-style graphics, shown over the channel by the TV launcher (`launcher/`, ClassicIntro) and
the phone app (`phone/tvhome-phone/`, PlayerIntro). Both apps take this folder in through their `build.gradle`
(`sourceSets`): the pages and their files (`assets/broadcast/`), the Java that plays them (`java/com/gathin/broadcast/`)
and its tests (`javatest/`).

| Part | What it is |
|---|---|
| `assets/broadcast/kickoff.html` | The kick-off pop-up: both crests fly in, the competition's 3D trophy rises behind, the bar opens with the teams, the tab changes (competition, Whitebrook Road Sports, the ground), then it all closes. Kinds (`Moments.KINDS`): `kickoff`, `restart` (after a goal: the score under the competition, then a beat later the tab turns to its scorer, a ball, the name, the minute and (og) or (pen) as the board marks them; no word for the moment), `second` (the second half, its tab turning to SECOND HALF), `full` (full time: the final score, its tab turning to FULL TIME). |
| `assets/broadcast/lineups.html` | The classic line-ups: each team by position, the players beaming in with their number and name, the subs, the formation on a tilted pitch; both teams, then a wipe back to the channel. Back fades it away. |
| `assets/broadcast/strap.html` | The first design's kick-off pop-up: a strap in the scoreboard's own look, every look the boards have (the first nine, the 3D kits, the Scoreboard Lab's five, the competitions' sixteen and the four retro boards; By competition's the game's own), the teams, the ground and its weather, that folds into the board where it sits (`board` {x, y, w, h} on 1920 x 1080). The competitions', retro and Lab boards are drawn as CompBug draws them (the same layout, lettering and logo cuts, as SVG), so the fold lands on the app's own board. Same kinds as the pop-up: after a goal the score (no word, the clock where the word was), then a beat later its scorer opening out on the line under the strap where the brand is (a ball, the name, the minute, (og) or (pen)), at full time FULL TIME with the final score and the clock at FT, as the board then shows it. |
| `assets/broadcast/studio.html` | The studio line-ups (the line-ups' third style): each team in turn beside a side panel in its competition's colours and logo (the club's crest, the position, its name up the panel), a title card with the club's kit turning in 3D (its captain's name and number on the back), then a page a position (the keeper, the defenders, the midfielders, the forwards) with each player's picture, rolling number, flag, captain's band and names, the family's swish as each page comes and a pluck a player, then the substitutes and the manager; the other team the same. Everything on one beat (124 a minute), so its music (`intros/sound-studio.m4a`, by `tools/studio_music.py`, no recordings) fits any formation; 45 s, the channel's sound lowered meanwhile. Drawn as compositor layers (`layers.js`), the shirt live in WebGL. Back fades it away. |
| `assets/broadcast/kits.html` | The kit pictures, out of sight: a side's shirt in 3D in the kit it wears, turned through the angles the app asks for, every frame on one sheet handed back (`java/.../KitSheets.java`): the scoreboards' "3D kits" look (each side's shirt in a cell of its own, turning in and swaying) and a goal's shirt (`KitTurn.java`: out of the board, round to the scorer's name and number, and home). |
| `assets/broadcast/stadium.html` | The first design's line-ups: a stadium drawn for the day and the weather, the Whitebrook Road Sports ident, a title card, each eleven on a formation board with their pictures, the manager and subs, the referee and a countdown, then a wipe back. Back fades it away. |
| `assets/broadcast/reveal.html` | The competition intros (`assets-tv/broadcast/intros/`, 20 s films made ahead from `src/reveal/reveal.js` by `tools/reveal_render.js`, their sound by `tools/reveal_synth.py`): the Premier League's is the family's own clip then the Whitebrook Road Sports ending; the others, F1, UFC, boxing and plain football (a Champions League ball) in the same style with their own logos. The app plays the sound itself and silences the channel meanwhile; the page writes the fixture under the wordmark; Back fades it away. |
| `intros/season-<league>.mp4` | The season intros (Premier League, LaLiga, Serie A, Bundesliga, Ligue 1): every club of the season in a scene of its own made from what is on its crest or what it is known by, a bar each of music made for them (`sound-season-<league>.m4a`, the ending's sound after it), then the Whitebrook Road Sports ending (`tail.mp4`) with the fixture. Played by `reveal.html` over an ad break from 90 to 6 minutes before a game of the league, else as its matchday intro. |
| `assets/broadcast/match.html` | The match intro, drawn live in WebGL (`match3d.js`: three.js and `src/match/`): an open in the style of a big network's football show, made our own, for the game about to be watched. A football comet past a tower of the kick-off time, the Whitebrook Road Sports lockup in chrome, then each club's part (a dive through its city of buildings in its colours with its name up their sides, its best-known player's name card, its crest in a neon diamond, a neon tunnel, its fans' cry three times), the competition's other clubs that day (tunnels, crests, two of the round's stars face to face in the Premier League), the two clubs face to face, their short names either side of a neon diamond, three players of each, and the end with the fixture; 48 s to its own music (`intros/sound-match.m4a`). A Champions League game also has the competition's own style (38 s, `intros/sound-match-ucl.m4a`): its mark among chrome ribbons, a white star among star-panelled balls, a hall of arches with the trophy, drapes for each club's crest and star, the two clubs either side of the star, the rise up the rocks to our 3D trophy and Whitebrook Road Sports with the fixture. No footage. Made ready out of sight; it measures this TV and draws smaller when it must. |
| `java/.../GoalReveal.java`, `ClubGoals.java`, `GoalShapes.java`, `GoalLooks.java` | The new goal animations, inside the scoreboard over the scorers' part of it (their name, the score and on to the far end; the whole board where a part makes no sense): a sweep in the club's own two colours from the scorers' name, their crest edge-on turning to face us and sliding beside their letters as the strip goes dark, what the club is known by coming in (an emoji drawn in the device's own emoji font as a silhouette in the club's colour, one of GoalShapes' silhouettes: a cannon, a gull, towers, a rose, a trident, a lighthouse, a pine cone, a submarine; or its fans' phrase: YNWA, HALA MADRID, BARÇA, MIA SAN MIA, ECHTE LIEBE, ICI C'EST PARIS, DROIT AU BUT, FINO ALLA FINE...), turning into GOAL! (its letters spread wide pulling together over a panel lit in the club's colours, a small burst), then the sweep again and the new score; 4.9 s, its timeline as data, nothing made per frame. ClubGoals: the 96 clubs of the five leagues (the season intros', by ESPN id, else by name: accents, FC/AFC/CF/SC, short forms), each with its sweep, GOAL!'s ink (WCAG 4.5 on both) and motif; any other team the generic (its colour from the board, a football). GoalLooks: the choice of goal animation, the same values and words in all three apps: As today (the default), GOAL band, Flip, Burst, 3D shirt, Club animations, Generic. |
| `java/.../Sfx.java` | The scoreboard's own sounds, played by the app: the line-ups' tab swish as anything slides out beside the board, the goal sound chosen over the board's goal animation, and the family's swish as its new score pops up. |
| `java/.../BroadcastView.java` | A see-through view playing one of them over the picture: the page in a WebView, its sounds (`sfx/`) in a SoundPool. |
| `java/.../BroadcastData.java` | What a page is given: clubs, crests, colours, competition, ground; for the line-ups FotMob's two teams with each player's picture, all inline. |
| `java/.../PlPhotos.java` | The Premier League's own player pictures, matched to FotMob's line-ups by club and name through its fantasy game's player list. |
| `java/.../LaLigaPhotos.java` | La Liga's own player pictures for a La Liga game, from each player's public page on laliga.com (checked for the same name and club), cut square to the Premier League's framing. |
| `java/.../MatchIntro.java` | The match intro's settings: the two clubs (ESPN's ids for the page's own club colours), each with its four best-known players (FotMob's squad by transfer value, the injured left out; in the line-ups' hour only the starters) in the Premier League's or La Liga's cut-out, else FotMob's face in a round portrait; the competition's other clubs that day (ESPN's scoreboard); two of the round's other stars in a Premier League game (its fantasy game's list). |
| `java/.../Moments.java` | When each comes: the line-ups from seven minutes before kick-off on the picture, the pop-up at each kick-off, 40 seconds after a goal reaches the picture (or once live play is back), and once at full time, as the board's own full time (Whistle) reaches the picture, never early. |
| `java/.../BoardSpec.java` | One of the family's own scoreboards (the scoreboard editor, My scoreboards): a small versioned JSON design read safely (anything bad or missing falls back, words kept legible, the panel solid), naming one of the nine palette looks as its layout, its colours (added time's red AddedTime.RED unless set), corners, border, finish, typeface (the bundled fonts), crests and strips. The boards draw it in its base look, so goals, pop-outs, added time and the strap (strap.js `S['custom']`) come with it. Its JSON schema for Ask AI. |
| `java/.../BoardDesigner.java`, `BoardEditing.java`, `BoardPaint.java`, `BoardColours.java`, `EditorStill.java`, `MyBoards.java` | Generate (designed boards from the clubs, the competition or a mood, held to TV contrast rules), the editor's working copy (the same on touch and the remote), the typeface and crest drawing, colour sums (WCAG contrast), the still and invented clubs the editor previews on, and My scoreboards' ids and names (synced as `myboard` items). |
| `java/.../Claude.java`, `AskAi.java`, `AiKey.java` | Ask AI: one Messages API request with the family's own key (plain HTTP and org.json, not the SDK, so all three apps take it with no new dependency), structured output held to the design's schema, the refusal fallback, stop_reason first, plain words for a wrong key, a busy service or no network; the scoreboard and logo prompts; the key in its own private preferences, never logged or synced. |
| `java/.../PicSources.java`, `PicQuery.java`, `SkyListing.java`, `PicLinks.java` | Programme pictures for Homefield TV's and the phone's TV guides, info cards and panels, found in one order: the guide's own (XMLTV's icon or image, PicLinks keeping them in a few bytes a programme), Sky's programme images (SkyListing: Sky's public schedule matched by channel and time), TMDB by the title (PicQuery: the title tidied, its year and episode, whether it is worth looking for, the answer matched with care), then one always there: a game's crests (ClubGoals' clubs, ESPN's crests), a picture for what the programme is (PicSources.kindOf: from its words, the guide's categories and the channel), else the channel's own. A programme's pictures are one chain of addresses, best first; each place takes the first large enough for it. Nothing here fetches. |
| `java/.../PicFinder.java`, `Catalogues.java`, `PicFetch.java`, `PicVet.java` | Each programme's own picture looked for the same way by both apps (PicFinder, off the main thread): the guide's own, then the programme found by its channel and time, its title the same, in the UK guide catalogues the public websites read (Catalogues: Sky's episode picture, Freeview's, Virgin Media's, Sky's season or series picture; each one's channels under its own names, HD, UK, regions and +1 tidied, a hand-kept table for the big UK channels), a game's own (TheSportsDB's free test key), then by title (TVMaze, and TMDB through each app's own key: the episode's still, then the series'). PicFetch keeps the requests apart per host with a plain user agent and short timeouts and keeps the answers on disk (listings for hours, titles for days); PicVet passes over placeholders (a known placeholder's address, a plain card such as Sky's "Image unavailable", a tiny picture, the channel's logo). |
| `java/.../PicPlace.java`, `PicArt.java` | Where a programme's picture goes in a cell, card, panel or band, the same in both apps (PicPlace, pure): covering a place close to its own shape, else the whole picture (or nearly) at the place's height beside the words, faces whole, never scaled past covering or enlarged past 1.5 times, gliding along a guide cell as it scrolls with no jumps (slide). PicArt draws it so (the place's own colour from the picture's edges drawn out behind it, its edges feathered: never a hard edge), and the made-up pictures: a kind's (a pitch, a court, a ring, a globe, a projector's beam... in code, no words, no people), a game's (two crests on their colours) and a channel's own colour. |
| `java/.../LogoSpec.java`, `LogoArt.java`, `LogoDesigner.java`, `LogoPacks.java` | A channel logo made in the app (a design spec drawn crisp and see-through from fixed shapes, marks and the bundled fonts; no image model), the offline wordmarks and monograms, and the logo catalogue (TV Logos and iptv-org's database searched by name; their licences in LogoPacks.LICENCES). |

The pages are built from `src/` with `./build.sh` (Node's `npx` fetches esbuild to put the 3D trophies and three.js
into one script):

- `src/kickoff/`, `src/lineups/`: the drawing code, from the mock-ups, set to draw over a see-through page (`POP.background = 'none'`, `window.OVERLAY`).
- `src/host/`: the pages and the small scripts that take the app's data (`BROADCAST.start(json)`) and drive them in real time, calling the app back (`AndroidBroadcast.ready/started/done/sound/log`).
- `src/vendor/`: three.js r170 (MIT) and its RoomEnvironment.
- `src/fonts/`: Khand 600 and 700 (the lettering matched to the user's clips), Barlow Condensed 700 (its `&`, and the Premier League's ribbons), Outfit (the Whitebrook Road Sports wordmark); and for the competitions' scoreboards (CompBug), each cut down to Latin, Montserrat 500 to 800, Inter 500 and 600, Rubik 600, Barlow Condensed 800 and Barlow Semi Condensed 700. All SIL Open Font Licence (`OFL.txt`, copied with them).
- `src/strap/`, `src/stadium/`: the first designs (the stadium continues `src/lineups/head.js`, the same ground).
- `src/kits/`: the 3D shirt (`kit3d.js`: the outline extruded, shaped, a collar, the kit painted on front and back with the crest, the name and the number; no maker's marks, no sponsor) and the kits (`designs.js`: the Premier League's twenty clubs' 2026/27 home shirts as the league's own player pictures show them, a few other clubs' patterns, else a plain shirt in the colours ESPN gives for the kit worn), `sheet.js` (kits.html's frames). `src/studio/studio.js`: the studio line-ups. `src/flags/`: the players' flags, from the open Twemoji set (Twitter, Inc and other contributors, CC-BY 4.0; `tools/flags.py` fetched them, named by FotMob's country codes).
- `src/reveal/reveal.js`: the intros' drawing (deterministic, `draw(t)`); `tools/reveal_comps.json` names each one's logo, colours and words; `tools/reveal_render.js` renders frames, `tools/reveal_synth.py` the sound (no recordings).
- `src/season/season.js`: the season intros' drawing (deterministic, `draw(t)`); `tools/season_clubs.py` writes `season_clubs.json`, each club's scene by ESPN id (background, the pictures and how they move); `tools/season_render.js` renders a league's frames from ESPN's list of its clubs and their crests; `tools/season_music.py` makes its music (no recordings, no one else's tune). The animals and things around the crests are from the open Twemoji set (Twitter, Inc and other contributors, CC-BY 4.0); the cannon, gulls, white rose, towers, spires, lighthouse, submarine, pine cone, trident and Eiffel tower are drawn in `season.js`.
- `src/match/`: the match intro (deterministic, `draw(t)`): `kit.js` (lettering, crests in chrome and neon, players lit in their club's colour, faces), `shots.js` (the network style's scenes), `ucl.js` (the Champions League style's), `match.js` (the timeline, the flashes at the cuts, the fans' cries), `clubs.json` (each club's colours by ESPN id, from `tools/season_clubs.json`). `tools/match_render.js` records it frame by frame for a look; `tools/match_music.py` makes both styles' music (no recordings, no one else's tune: not the competition's anthem). `match3d.js` is built on its own with esbuild (`build.sh`'s last step; `build.sh` as a whole also remakes the other pages from `src/`, so check `git status` after it).
- `src/sfx/`: the sounds, synthesised for the mock-ups and cut to each moment (Ogg Vorbis): `popup-*`, `swish-*`, `strap-<look>` (no whistle, no crowd), `stadium-*`, `studio-*` (`tools/studio_music.py`: whooshes, a stab and plucks in each team's key; `studio-swish` is the family's own swish), and the goal sounds `goal-*` (nine made by `tools/goal_synth.py`, timed to the board's GOAL band; three of them in the style measured from the family's kick-off clip, a bright swipe of air into a deep boom, with nothing of its sound used; six more by `tools/reveal_goal_synth.py`, timed to the new goal animations' stages so their hits land on the sweep, the crest's turn, the motif, GOAL!'s burst and the sweep off: Whoosh and ting, Brass swell, Crowd rise, Riser and drop, Bells, Thunder and sparkle; 48 kHz, matched in loudness to the others). The Scoreboard Lab moments' sounds `lab-*` (made by `tools/lab_synth.py`, quiet on purpose, under the tab swish): the net shivering after a goal, the crest bursting, the score's odometer roll, the digit's flip, the red card's swing, VAR's check and a substitution's crossing. `goal-swoosh` and `score-swish` are the family's own picks, stock sound effects they supplied: the swoosh over the GOAL band (cut to start 0.8 s in, its loudest moment 0.65 s in, as the band lands; 3 dB down) and the swish as the new score pops (trimmed to its whoosh, its loudest moment 0.5 s in).

Nothing here is the broadcaster's: the drawing, the trophies (our own 3D models), the lettering (a free font close to
theirs) and the sounds are all made for this project; the competitions' and clubs' logos come from the scores' feeds
at run time, as elsewhere in the apps.

Without WebGL the pop-up draws flat trophies instead. If FotMob has no line-ups yet, the apps ask again a minute later.
