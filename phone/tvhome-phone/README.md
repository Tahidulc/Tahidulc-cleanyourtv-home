# Homefield: the TV Home app for Android phones and tablets

The launcher's own parts on a phone: Home with the scores box and weather, Watch (Discover, Movies and TV shows, a
title's page, the Episode map, Coming up, Because you watched, Film night, trailers), Sports (Match Centre, tables, F1,
This Weekend), alerts as phone notifications with the Control Centre's switches, and Live TV for the family's own M3U
playlist or Xtream Codes login. It pairs with the TVs as Delivery to TV does and keeps My list, ratings, the Film
night plan, Coming up's mutes, reminders and starred clubs the same everywhere (SYNC.md).

Full screen, a tap brings up an info tile that suits what's on (the Full-screen TV Info Tiles canvas): Match night for a
game (the scores box with both crests and the competition's logo, the fixture, kick-off or the score, each club's
form or the game so far), Cinematic for a film, Card for a series' episode and Glass ribbon for the rest, each with
the channel's logo, and facts in turn while a game is on (its numbers, the table, each side's run, earlier meetings,
top scorers). The player's options can set one style for everything, or the guide panel as before.

The scores box is the TV's, as at first: the competition's logo on its navy block, each club's crest on its own white
tile, and the blue middle with the score (or the kick-off time) and under it the clock live in minutes and seconds,
going on second by second between ESPN's reads (67:23; 90:00 with +4 on a red chip in added time), or HT, FT or
KICK-OFF.
Full screen, the picture goes into a small window as you leave the app (from the guide too; the player's options turn
that off), and a phone on its side stretches the picture to the screen. The info tile is Small at first (on its side a
card at the foot's left, a little over half the width, so the game shows; upright, in the room under the picture),
Medium or Large in the player's options, which also set how long it stays (4, 8, 15 or 30 seconds, or until a swipe
down takes it away). A game's tile and its page have "Also Showing On", from the family's own playlists: the channels
whose guide has the game and the playlist's channels of the broadcasters it is on, each with its logo, the likeliest
first and marked Best match; a channel's 4K/UHD version is listed too, with a "4K" mark on its logo (4K channels in Live
TV wear the same mark, and one with no logo of its own shows its channel's, Sky's, matched past "UK:" and "UHD").
Broadcasters the playlist has none of are named only when it has nothing for the game.

Players have live ratings out of ten (Ratings), worked out from ESPN's figures for them in the game: everyone on starts
at 6.4, goals, assists, shots on target and fouls won lift it, fouls, offsides, cards and own goals bring it down, a
keeper's saves and a clean sheet count, and the score moves it a touch. They show by each name on the line-ups pitch
(the best with a star) and the substitutes, and the full-screen tile's facts name each side's top rated.

A game's alerts come as cards: folded, what happened and who with the scoring side's crest; opened out, both crests on
white discs, the score with the scoring side's number lit, the minute, and the 90 minutes with each goal on them. Under
each, and under the kick-off and line-ups alerts too: Watch match (the game in Live TV, on the playlist channel that
"Also Showing On" puts first), Match and Mute game. "Following live" draws the live
games as the scores box's rows. A game's page has the header card (crests, score or kick-off, the clock, the scorers,
where it is on, Remind me), then Timeline (the game on a pitch, each shot, goal and card where it happened, the newest
pulsing, then every moment by its minute), Line-ups (both elevens on one pitch by their formations, the substitutes
under it, with the players' photos) and Stats in each side's colours. The line-ups come as both elevens on a pitch,
an F1 result as its podium, and "Following live" as the scoreboards with the first game's clock going on.

Films and series play only from your own playlists: Play looks in every playlist in use (its films and series, Vod:
matched by TMDB's id when the provider gives one, else by name and year; a series asks for its season and episode) and
plays it in the app; the line under the title says which playlists have it. Nothing else is looked for and no other
app is opened: when none of your playlists has a title, it says so. Live TV and those films and series play only the
playlists you give it; the app ships with no channels or sources. The tabs are Home, Sports, Live TV, Watch and
Settings.

The TV guide is dark: the chosen category as the title with round buttons, the categories as dark pills (a flag
where one names a country), "Today" with the times across and a white mark at now, and each channel a row of rounded
cards: its logo on a tile in the logo's own colour, each programme a block in a deep colour of its own with what has
been shown a shade darker, and the channel's name with its picture's tag (HD, FHD, 4K, UHD) and each start time over
the titles. Now opens just after the logos, with room for what has been shown.

A live game's clock is in minutes and seconds wherever its score is shown (the lists, the game's page, the match
centre, the TV guide's sport rows, the player's tile and small window), going on second by second; the notification
and the widget tick it with Android's own clock under the hour, and show the drawn clock after it (Android's turns
into hours there).

The scoreboard while you watch (on in the player; Options, Scoreboard): the channel's game over the picture, read
every 15 seconds and shown as the channel shows it. The channel's delay (the alerts' "It's just happened" or its
chips) holds the score and the clock back, so the score changes as the goal goes in on the screen; once the channel's
own clock has been seen turning a minute, it keeps time with that to the second. On a game it finds the channel's own
scoreboard on the picture (still edges where the picture moves, its clock's digits flipping) and covers it at the
same size: one row when it is wide, two when it is nearer square. On an F1 channel it shows the session in step the
same way: the race's lap, practice's time left, qualifying's part with its time left, and the first three. On any
other channel, or a film, it shows a followed club's game on now. Goals and the other football alerts, F1 and other
sport, and breaking news (BBC News's and BBC Sport's new lead stories) drop from under it, each switched on or off
there or in Settings. It can sit over other apps too (Settings, or the player's Options: Android's "Display over
other apps" is asked for), dragged where it suits, tap for the next game, hold to stop, with how far behind that
app's picture runs set in Settings.

The scoreboard reads the channel's own scoreboard off the picture, on the phone (Google's offline text reader):
its clock, so the board shows the same time to the second, and its score, so the board's changes as the channel's
does (and the channel's delay is set from it, holding the alerts to the picture too). On an F1 channel it reads the
timing tower's lap ("LAP 23/57") or time left. It has five looks to choose from (Classic, Glass, Light, Club colours,
Minimal), and where the channel's logo is found on the picture it covers it with Homefield's own mark: Badge, Glass,
Mark only or Wordmark, or leaves it (Settings and the player's Options). The Live TV tab's programme panel is black.

Pop-ups over apps, as the TV's: an alert drops in at the top of the screen over whatever app is open, a black card
with its coloured label, the competition, the drawn card (the goal's, the line-ups, F1's podium) or its words, and
Watch, Match or Read. Swipe it away left, right or up, or tap it for the game; it goes by itself after a few seconds.
Settings, Pop-ups over apps: on or off (Android's "Display over other apps" is asked for), which kinds pop up (a chip
each), and one to try. Its notification still comes, with its sound but no banner over the pop-up. Breaking news is
an alert like the rest now (Settings, News and weather).

One scoreboard, in step with the picture, everywhere (3.9): the player's board shows what the channel's own scoreboard
shows. It finds it by reading the top of the picture for this game's clock (not a time of day), then copies it every
few seconds: the clock to the second (46:12 in added time, stopped when the channel's stops, its "+4"), the score once
it has read it twice, and HT and FT, so it is never ahead of the picture. Options says what it last read. Portrait,
picture-in-picture and the channel's tile show the same board and time, the tile's stats in step with it. The "Following
live" notification counts in minutes and seconds. In the full-screen player, the menu (⋮) tests everything the app
can show, now on the channel playing, whatever the styles chosen and without changing them: each kind of alert (all in
turn, or one) and the line-ups', the kick-off pop-up in both styles at the kick-off, the restart after a goal and the
second half, the line-ups in both styles, the scoreboard's goal for each side with the goal sound chosen (and each goal
sound to hear), its scorer, stats, line-up card, a substitution, VAR and the added time's +4, each info tile, and the
other notifications and on-screen moments. Settings, Test notifications, has those that need no channel playing. A test
is never kept in the alerts and never takes a real one's place. Overlays guard against burn-in: they move a little every
minute and dim after five. Broadcast mode (Settings): when the channel goes to its adverts before kick-off, at half
time or after the game, Homefield shows its own break (your reminders, news, what's on in each section, trailers) with
the channel small in a corner, and goes back when the channel does. Swipe up on the Live TV panel to hide it and see
more of the guide; swipe down for it.

Broadcast mode's break, more of it (4.0): fun facts (Bangladesh first, two for every one of the world's or football's,
fresh each break), a quiz question with its answer a few seconds on, today's prayer times from Darus Salam Mosque
(each prayer's beginning and its jamā'ah, the Islamic date, the next one counting down; in Ramadan, iftar or sehri;
worked out for Manchester when the mosque's page can't be read), and Today (the date, the Islamic date, the weather
and sunset). Settings, What the break shows: a chip for each kind. The live window in the corner is the picture itself
now, not the bars round it, so upright it no longer fills half the screen.

Full screen stays on its side (4.1): turning the phone on its side in Live TV opens the channel full screen, and now it
stays full screen, either way round, until you go back or tap the cross. Lying down with the phone, or tilting it,
no longer turns it upright and drops you back in the guide. To have turning upright go back again, the player's
Options has "Turn upright to leave full screen".

Homefield in the corner, and the guide from full screen (4.2): the Live TV tab's top left has Homefield's mark and
name (made smaller if it must, so it always fits beside the buttons) over the number of channels; the category
chosen is the lit chip under the picture. In full screen, the information panel has "TV guide": it goes to the Live
TV guide, the channel going on in the picture at its top, wherever full screen was opened from.

The name in the corner always fits (4.3): Homefield's name beside its mark, and the channel count under it, are
sized as the top bar is laid out, to the room left by the Now and other buttons: as large as fits (smaller with a
larger display text), never cut off or under a button; where even a small name has no room, the mark stands alone.

Live TV opens where you left it (4.4): the guide opens on the category you chose last (All, Favourites, Sport or
one of the playlist's own, while the playlist still has it), and the channel you watched last plays again in the
guide's picture as the tab opens, or as you come back to the app. Close the picture with ✕ and it waits instead,
paused until tapped, until you next play something.

Recently watched on Home (4.5): under the scores box, your clubs' games and the latest alerts, which stay at the
top and show at once, comes everything watched on this phone, newest first and all kinds together: films and
episodes from your playlists (the poster, how far in, or the next episode after one watched to its end), the games
watched on Live TV (both crests and the score as it is now or as it ended, LIVE while it is on) and other
programmes (the channel's logo, the programme, News, Film or TV). A channel counts once it has played a minute, so
flicking past doesn't add it. Tap plays it again from where it was left, or goes back to the channel; a game that
has finished opens its match page. Hold one to take it off. It is kept on this phone only, and goes when the
playlists are removed.

Full screen upright or on its side, the small window a tap away, and the marks with their logos (4.6): the guide's
full screen button (or tapping the picture again, or swiping it up) opens full screen upright, with the picture
across the width. Turn the phone on its side there and it goes on its side, and back upright again. Turning the
phone on its side on the guide while a channel plays goes full screen on its side, even with the phone's rotation
locked. On its side, a "Picture in picture" button comes up under the buttons at the top right for a few seconds
(and again with a tap): one press and the channel goes into the small window. The information panel's picture and
sound marks now sit under the programme's title, where they fit, and on to a second line rather than out of the box:
4K UHD, 1080p HD and the rest in words, Dolby Vision, HDR10, HDR10+, Dolby Atmos and Dolby as their logos (the TVs'),
the channels (5.1) beside Dolby's, and S for subtitles. They show on the film and series panels too, upright as
well as on its side, and the same marks are on the film and episode player's information bar.

The picture's quality as icons too (4.7): the information panel's and the film player's picture mark is an icon
now, as Dolby's and HDR10's are: 4K Ultra HD, HD 1080p, HD 720p, HD (before the stream has said which) and SD, each a
white chip with its letters cut out and its words beside it, and HDR HLG the same way. They sit in the same outlined
boxes as the rest, one height for all.

Picture in picture takes you off the app (4.8): the picture in picture button at the top right of full screen
(and Picture in picture in the options) puts the channel in the small window and goes straight to the phone's home
screen, so the picture shows over other apps without a swipe away. The film and episode player's button does the
same. The separate Picture in picture button that came up on its side in 4.6 has gone.

The scoreboard over the channel's no longer closes the stream (4.9): the work that finds the channel's own
scoreboard, reads its clock and covers it keeps any trouble it meets to itself (a frame it can't read is let go and
the next one taken), so the picture plays on whatever happens there. One case could end the player: a frame still
being read when the game was found (or the small window opened) was added to a finder already let go. If the app
ever does close unexpectedly now, the next time it opens it says so once, with Copy details: the kind of error and
where in the app it happened (never its words, which could hold a stream's address), to send on.

The scoreboard and the logo where you want them, kept still (4.10): hold the scoreboard (or Homefield's mark over
the channel's logo), or choose "Move and resize the scoreboard and logo" in the player's options, and both are
outlined to drag with a finger. The panel along the foot makes each smaller or bigger, picks the scoreboard's look
(Classic, Glass, Light, Club colours, Minimal) and the logo's (Badge, Glass, Mark only, Wordmark, or the channel's
own), and has Keep them still, Reset (back to where Homefield puts them) and Done. The places are kept for that
channel, upright and on its side alike; with no game on, a made-up one shows to place. The options also have the
scoreboard's size in its own corner (Small, Medium, Large, Extra large). Keep the scoreboard and logo still (on at
first, in the options and in Settings): they no longer move a pixel or two against burn-in (they still dim after five
minutes without a change), and once over the channel's own scoreboard and logo they stay there while the game is on.

The scoreboard no longer closes the app (4.11): the first time the player read the channel's own scoreboard (its clock
and score), one of the patterns it reads them with failed to start on the phone, as a phone reads such patterns more
strictly than a computer does, and the app stopped. It now starts on any phone, and the app's tests read every pattern
in it for that mistake, so it cannot come back.
